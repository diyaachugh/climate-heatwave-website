Prediction is what turns raw weather data into a warning people can act on days in advance, rather than a headline once the heat has already arrived.

Approach

How prediction works here

Climate Intelligence combines live weather observations, satellite data, and historical climate records, then runs them through a machine learning model trained to recognise the conditions that precede a heatwave. Forecasting the risk before it becomes an emergency gives governments and communities time to prepare rather than just react.

Data Used

Surface & air temperature

The core signal — sustained readings above the seasonal average are the first indicator of a developing heatwave.

Humidity

Combined with temperature, humidity determines how the heat actually feels and how dangerous it is to the body.

Wind speed

Low wind speeds let hot air settle and linger, while stronger winds can help disperse it.

Solar radiation

Direct measurement of incoming sunlight helps refine how quickly surfaces and air will heat up.

Historical records

Past heatwave patterns for a region help the model recognise what "abnormal" looks like locally.

Satellite observations

Wide-area imagery fills in the gaps between ground stations, especially over less-monitored regions.

Prediction Process

01

Collect

Gather weather data from ground stations and satellites in real time.

02

Clean & process

Remove noise and errors, then structure the raw data for the model.

03

Train

Train a machine learning model on historical heatwave patterns.

04

Predict

Estimate heatwave risk for the coming days based on current conditions.

05

Alert

Generate an early warning for the areas and populations most at risk.

Why this order matters: a prediction is only as good as the data behind it — which is why cleaning and processing comes before training, and training comes before any prediction is trusted enough to send out as a warning.
